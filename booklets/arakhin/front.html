About this edition

What is inside

  • The verses. Every verse the masechta builds on, in full, with vowels and ta'amim. The heart of it is all of Vayikra 27 and Vayikra 25:8–34. Each group of verses is followed by a short note saying which mishnayos use it and how.
  • The Mishnah. All 9 chapters and 50 mishnayos with vowels. Each mishnah is followed by the complete commentary of R' Ovadiah of Bartenura in Rashi script, then by notes. The heading of each mishnah gives the daf where the Gemara takes it up, and each chapter heading gives its Daf Yomi dates.
  • The notes. They are meant to add to Bartenura, not repeat him. They bring the main points of Tosafos Yom Tov (R' Yom Tov Lipmann Heller), Tiferes Yisroel (R' Yisroel Lipschitz: Yachin, the running commentary, and Boaz, the longer discussions), and Rambam's Peirush HaMishnayos, along with Rambam's rulings in Hilchos Arachin. Melechet Shlomo and Rashi on Chumash are brought where they help. They note where these commentators disagree with Bartenura or with each other, and where the halachah is disputed.

Sources and how the text was checked

All Hebrew texts were downloaded from Sefaria. The Mishnah and Bartenura are the vocalized Torat Emet editions. Tosafos Yom Tov, Yachin, Boaz and Melechet Shlomo are from the Romm Vilna Mishnah (1913). Rambam's commentary is the Hebrew translation of the Vilna edition.

The verses are Sefaria's Miqra according to the Masorah, which is based on the Aleppo Codex. Each of the 84 verses was compared letter by letter with a second, independent edition (Tanach with Ta'amei Hamikra, based on the Leningrad Codex). They agree throughout except for parashah markers and two spellings with and without a vav/yud. Devarim 22:14 reads וְהוֹצִא where the other edition has והוציא. Bamidbar 13:32 reads וַיֹּצִיאוּ where the other has ויוציאו. The Aleppo-based spelling is printed. The one ketiv/kri in Arachin's verses, Vayikra 25:30 אשר לא חומה, is discussed in Mishnah 9:6.

About the notes. The English notes were written by Claude, an AI model, from the commentaries named above. They are a study aid. They are not a translation of those works and not a halachic ruling. Please check the original inside before relying on a point.

Transliteration

Hebrew terms are given in Ashkenazi pronunciation (Tosafos, mishnayos, bedek habayis). References such as "(4:1)" point to a chapter and mishnah of this masechta.

The masechta at a glance מסכת ערכין

Arachin, in Seder Kodashim, opens with a vow most people never think about: "ערכי עלי", "my valuation is upon me". Someone who says this, or pledges another person's valuation, owes the Temple's upkeep fund (bedek habayis) a fixed sum set by the Torah according to the person's age and sex (Vayikra 27:1–8). What the person would be worth on the market makes no difference. "דמי עלי" is different: it pledges a person's actual market value, as if sold as a slave. Much of the masechta turns on this contrast between a fixed erech and an assessed damim.

From valuations of people the masechta moves on through the rest of Vayikra 27 and the laws of Yovel in Vayikra 25:

  • Consecrated fields: the fixed redemption price of an inherited field (sedeh achuzah), "fifty shekels for a chomer of barley seed", reduced year by year until Yovel. A purchased field (sedeh mikneh) is treated differently. An unredeemed field passes to the kohanim at Yovel.
  • Cherem: devoting property, and whether an unspecified cherem goes to the kohanim or to bedek habayis.
  • Sales under Yovel: fields that return at Yovel, houses in walled cities that become the buyer's permanently after a year, unwalled villages, and the special status of the Levite cities.

Along the way the Mishnah gathers several lists. Chapter 2 sets out the minimums and maximums of the Temple service: trumpet blasts, flutes, lyres, the twelve leviim on the duchan, the calendar. Chapter 3 lists the Torah's fixed fines and draws its lesson about lashon hara: "the one who speaks with his mouth is worse than the one who acts". The Gemara expands that into its famous sugya (15a–16b).

A poor person pays what he can afford, but never less than one shekel (2:1). Age is fixed at the time of the vow, and the fifth, twentieth and sixtieth years count with the bracket below (4:4). Under one month there is no erech at all (1:1).

Key terms

ערך erech
The fixed Torah valuation of a person by age and sex. "ערך פלוני עלי" or "ערכי עלי".
דמים damim
A person's market value as a slave. "דמי פלוני עלי". It must be assessed, and "אין דמים למתים" (5:2).
מעריך / נערך ma'arich / ne'erach
The one who pledges an erech / the one whose erech is pledged.
נודר / נידר noder / nidar
The one who vows damim / the one whose damim are vowed. The Mishnah also calls the ma'arich "נודר", after the verse.
השג יד hasagas yad
"What his hand can reach": a poor pledger pays according to his means (4:1).
הקדש · בדק הבית hekdesh · bedek habayis
Consecrated property. Unspecified arachin and damim go to the Temple-upkeep fund.
גזבר gizbar
The Temple treasurer, who sells or redeems hekdesh and takes pledges.
חומש chomesh
The fifth an owner adds when redeeming his own hekdesh.
שדה אחוזה sedeh achuzah
An inherited field. Fixed redemption price. If not redeemed, it goes to the kohanim at Yovel.
שדה מקנה sedeh mikneh
A purchased field. Redeemed at its value, and returns to the original owner at Yovel.
יובל Yovel
The fiftieth year. In force only while all the tribes live in the land (8:1).
גרעון כסף gir'on kesef
The reduction of the price by the years already used.
חרם cherem
Property devoted to the kohanim (charmei kohanim, never redeemed) or to bedek habayis (redeemable).
משכון mashkon
Collateral taken by the gizbar from one who owes arachin (5:6, 6:3).
קנוניא kenunya
Collusion to recover consecrated property, e.g. by a sham divorce (6:1).
בתי ערי חומה batei arei chomah
Houses in cities walled since Yehoshua's time. Redeemable only within a year.
בתי החצרים batei chatzerim
Houses in unwalled villages. They have the advantages of both houses and fields (9:7).
מגרש migrash
The open belt around a city, which may not be changed (9:8).

Before you start

Arachin stands largely on its own and is generally reckoned one of the easier tractates of Kodashim. It is short and has little sacrificial procedure. What helps most is a working sense of hekdesh and its redemption with the added fifth, the coins above, and the basics of Yovel and Shemittah. The harder stretches are arithmetical: the field-redemption sums (chapters 7–8), the counting of ages (4:4), and in the Gemara the Yovel and Temple-period calculations around 12a–13b.

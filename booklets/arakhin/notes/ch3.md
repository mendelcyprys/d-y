# intro
"**יש בערכין להקל ולהחמיר**" collects the five places where the Torah fixes a sum regardless of the real value:
- *erech*: 50 sela'im
- *sedeh achuzah*: 50 shekels per *beis kor*
- an ox that kills a slave: 30 sela'im
- *ones* and *mefateh*: 50 sela'im
- *motzi shem ra*: 100 sela'im

Rambam explains "lenient and strict" this way: sometimes a person pays more and sometimes less than the real value of what he vowed or did, because the Torah fixed the amount and did not leave it to assessment. The perek ends with the lesson the Gemara builds into its long treatment of **lashon hara** (15a–16b).

# 3:1
**Erech.** Someone who pledges the *erech* of the most beautiful man in Israel pays only 50 sela'im, even if that man would fetch a hundred *maneh* on the market. That is the leniency. Someone who pledges the *erech* of the ugliest man, even one disfigured and worth nothing, also pays 50. That is the stringency. Both cases assume the person is aged 20 to 60. *Damim* are different: "דמיו עלי" pays his real market value.

**Why "שבישראל"?** It does not mean a nochri is never valued. That would contradict R' Meir in 1:2. The Gemara (quoted by Tosafos Yom Tov) gives two answers. The Mishnah teaches incidentally that one may not say "how beautiful is this idolater", from לא תחנם. Or it said "Israel" because the next clause, *sedeh achuzah*, can only apply to a Jew.

# 3:2
**Sedeh achuzah.** The Mishnah contrasts a sandy field near the coast (חולת המחוז) with the fine orchards of **Sebaste** (Shomron). Bartenura offers two readings of *chulas hamechoz*: the trampled land around a town, or a place with poor fields. Yachin explains it as seashore sand. Either way, the redemption price is the same: **50 shekels per *beis zera chomer se'orim***, an area sown with a *kor* (30 *se'ah*) of barley. Yachin gives the area as about **75,000 square *amos***, roughly 274 × 274 *amos*. The price applies at the start of a Yovel cycle. After that, a sela and a *pundyon* is deducted for each year that has passed (7:1). **Trees** are redeemed separately at their value, and the land then at the fixed rate (Bartenura, Rambam). Rocky land that cannot be sown is redeemed at its actual value (Yachin).

**Sedeh mikneh** is a purchased field. It is redeemed **at its value**, because the verse says מכסת הערכך (27:23), and *michsah* means a counted sum of money (cf. והרמותם מכס, Bamidbar 31). **R' Eliezer** derives from the *gezeirah shavah* וחשב / וחשב that both fields have the fixed price. In his view the only difference is that the owner redeeming his *achuzah* adds a **fifth**, but there is no fifth on a *mikneh*, which is compared to *arachin*, and *arachin* carry no fifth. The halachah does not follow R' Eliezer.

Tosafos (quoted in Tosafos Yom Tov and Yachin) points out another difference R' Eliezer could have named. A consecrated *achuzah* redeemed by someone else goes to the **kohanim** at Yovel. A *mikneh* goes back to its **original owner**. The Mishnah named only the difference that applies when the owner himself redeems.

# 3:3
**The ox that killed a slave.** A *shor mu'ad* (an ox with a record of goring) that kills a slave costs its owner **30 sela'im** (Shemos 21:32), whether the slave was the finest or the poorest. If it kills a **free man**, the *kofer* is the **victim's** value: ונתן פדיון נפשו (Bartenura). If it only **injured** either one, the owner pays full damages, the difference in the victim's value before and after.

Why does the Mishnah say *mu'ad*? Even R' Akiva, who holds that a *tam* (an ox with no such record) that injures a person pays full damages (Bava Kamma 3:8), would agree with the ruling on injury. But there is no *kofer* or 30 sela'im for a *tam* that kills, so the killing case requires a *mu'ad* (Gemara, Tosafos Yom Tov).

# 3:4
**Ones and mefateh** (rape and seduction of a *na'arah*). The fine is **50 sela'im** (Devarim 22:29; Shemos 22:16, כמהר הבתולות). It is the same whether she is the most distinguished daughter of kohanim or from the lowliest Israelite family. Since the verse says 50 are paid "for the pleasure of lying", there must be other payments as well. These are **boshes** (shame) and **pegam** (loss of value) (Gemara, Tosafos Yom Tov).

**Pegam** is assessed as if she were a maidservant. How much more would a master pay for a virgin than for a non-virgin, to marry her to a slave he favours (Bartenura; Rambam, Na'arah 2)? **Boshes** follows "the one who shames and the one who is shamed". Shame from a lowly man is greater, and the victim's shame is measured by her standing. Yachin applies this phrase to *pegam* too. Tosafos Yom Tov applies it to *boshes* alone.

Boaz challenges Tosafos (Kesubos 40b), who held a distinguished girl's *pegam* must reflect her standing. Her dignity has already been paid for under *boshes*, so *pegam* can be priced like a maidservant's. That is how Rambam rules.

# 3:5
**Motzi shem ra.** A husband who falsely claims his bride was not a virgin pays **100 sela'im** (Devarim 22:19). Rambam adds that he receives *malkus*. The Mishnah draws the lesson: "**האומר בפיו יתר מן העושה מעשה**". The man who only *said* she was not a virgin pays 100. The man who actually *took* her virginity pays 50.

The Gemara asks (Tosafos Yom Tov): perhaps the 100 is because he exposed her to the death penalty (ואם אמת היה הדבר)? The answer comes from the verse: כי הוציא שם רע, so the penalty is for the bad name itself.

**The proof from the *midbar*.** The decree on our fathers was sealed only for **lashon hara**: the spies' report, ויציאו דבת הארץ (Bamidbar 13:32, Rambam). This was so even though they had tested Hashem "זה עשר פעמים". The word **זה** teaches that *this* sin alone was what sealed the decree, not the sum of the earlier ones (Rashi, Yachin). Yachin notes that they slandered only **land**, which feels no shame. The Gemara (15a) draws the *kal vachomer*: how much worse is slandering a person. The Gemara goes on (15a–16b) with its long discussion of lashon hara. It says lashon hara "kills three": the speaker, the listener, and the one spoken about. It also discusses *avak lashon hara* (the "dust" of lashon hara: remarks that hint at it) and *tochachah*.

# intro
Perek 6 deals with **collection**. It covers how beis din sells orphans' or hekdesh land: appraisal followed by public announcement. It covers hekdesh property that has a *kesubah* or a creditor's lien on it, and the suspicion of **kenunya** (collusion) against hekdesh. It then sets out what is **left to a debtor** when the gizbar takes pledges for *arachin* (the Torah's ואם מך **הוא**: let *him* live). Last, it covers what never belonged to him: the clothing of his wife and children.

# 6:1
**Appraisal and announcement.** When beis din sells orphans' land, it appraises the land and announces the sale for **30 consecutive days**. If it prefers, it announces for **60 days on Mondays and Thursdays** only, which is better. That comes to only 18 days of announcement (Rashi's count), but the longer spread reaches more people (Bartenura, Rambam). Hekdesh land is announced for **60 consecutive days**. Bartenura and Rambam: this refers to a *sedeh mikneh*, redeemed at its value. Yachin: also an *achuzah* when Yovel is not in force. The announcements are made **morning and evening**, when the workers go out to the fields and when they return. A buyer can ask them in the morning to look at the field and ask them in the evening what they saw. The announcement states the field's boundaries, its yield, its appraised value, and **what debt the sale is for**. Some buyers prefer paying a widow little by little. Others prefer a merchant creditor, who does not object to worn coins.

**When does beis din sell orphans' property at all?** The rule is "אין נזקקין לנכסי יתומים". The exceptions are a **kesubah**, which is to the orphans' benefit since it ends the widow's right to maintenance from their estate, a **nochri creditor** whose interest is mounting, and a debt known to be **unpaid**. A debt counts as unpaid if the father admitted it on his deathbed, died under a *niddui* for it, or it was not yet due (Bartenura, Rambam, Yachin). Tosafos Yom Tov adds the case of the *shor mu'ad*. Announcement applies only to **land**. Slaves are not announced, in case they hear and run away. *Shtaros* and movables are not, in case they are stolen while people gather to inspect them. Tosafos Yom Tov questions the "oath" mentioned by Bartenura and Rambam. Rambam himself rules in Hilchos Malveh 11 that these are collected without an oath.

**Hekdesh and the *kesubah*.** Someone who consecrated all his property while owing his wife's *kesubah*, then divorced her: **R' Eliezer** says he must make a **vow forbidding her to benefit from him**, so that he cannot remarry her. The vow is made *al da'as rabim* ("with the public in mind"), which cannot be annulled. The fear is that the divorce was a ploy to recover the money from hekdesh through her *kesubah*. **R' Yehoshua** says this is not necessary.

Bartenura and Rambam say everyone agrees a dying man does not collude against hekdesh, "for a man does not sin with nothing to gain", and that a healthy man might. They disagree about **she'eilah**, having the *hekdesh* annulled by a chacham. R' Eliezer follows Beis Shammai: *hekdesh* made in error still stands, so the man has no way out but a ploy. R' Yehoshua follows Beis Hillel: he could simply ask a chacham, so we do not suspect a ploy. The halachah follows **R' Yehoshua** (Bartenura). Tosafos Yom Tov sets out the other side. Rambam's code and Kesef Mishneh rule like **R' Eliezer**, reading "**אף** הערב" so that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel supports him. The Rosh agrees. An accurate text of Rambam's commentary says: "Although R' Yehoshua's view is true, the halachah follows R' Eliezer." Tosafos Yom Tov explains this as stringency for hekdesh, following Rav Huna.

**Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel:** the same applies to a **guarantor** of a *kesubah*. If the husband has no money and the guarantor must pay, the husband must first vow her away, in case he plans to remarry her and enjoy the guarantor's money. Yachin argues for ruling like R' Eliezer and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Even though annulment is possible, a chacham might not agree to hear him and three laymen might not be available.

# 6:2
**Debts on consecrated property.** The divorce or loan came **before** the *hekdesh*, so there is no suspicion of collusion. The wife and creditor still cannot collect **from hekdesh** directly. People would say hekdesh leaves without redemption. Instead "the redeemer redeems on condition of paying the wife her *kesubah* and the creditor his debt". The *hekdesh* never really took hold of what was owed to them. Rashi and Bartenura say he redeems for a trifle, such as a dinar. Tosafos holds he pays hekdesh the **excess** of the property's value over the debt. The creditor and the wife collect only **after an oath**, as anyone collecting from encumbered property must (Rambam, Arachin 6; Shevuos 42b). Yachin explains why the rule "no oath for hekdesh" does not apply here.

**Consecrated 90 and owed 100.** He **adds a dinar** and redeems on that condition. The point is still that hekdesh is not seen leaving without redemption. Who adds the dinar? Rashi says the creditor lends one more. Tosafos says the borrower borrows one more and redeems. Tosafos Yom Tov reconciles the two. We assume the creditor lent with *this* land in mind, as long as the land is worth at least **half** the debt. No one buys land at more than twice its value (Bartenura). Below half, he lent on trust and cannot collect from the hekdesh. Yachin, from the Tosefta, reads it the other way: below half, no dinar is needed, since no one would mistake it for hekdesh leaving free. Rambam rules *hekdesh* removes a lien (*hekdesh mafki'a mi-yedei shibud*). Yachin discusses whether this applies to *kedushas damim*.

# 6:3
**What is left to the debtor.** Even though we take pledges for *arachin*, we leave him **30 days' food, 12 months' clothing, a made-up bed, sandals and tefillin**. If he lacks them we leave him money to buy them (Bartenura). The source is ואם מך **הוא** מערכך: let *him* keep enough to live from the *erech* money. That is "him, but not his wife or children", even though he must support them (Rambam, Tosafos Yom Tov). Yachin adds that what he has already given them is not taken (6:5).

A **craftsman** keeps **two of each kind of tool**. A carpenter keeps two adzes and two saws, so that if one breaks he can keep working with the other (Yachin). Tosafos Yom Tov adds that these are examples: whatever tools that trade really needs. **R' Eliezer:** a farmer keeps his **team of oxen**, and a donkey-driver his **donkey**. The halachah does not follow him. These are **property**, not tools. Kesef Mishneh notes this overrides the earlier *stam* Mishnah in 4:3, since a *stam* followed by a dispute does not decide the halachah.

# 6:4
If he has **many of one tool and few of another**, for example three adzes and one saw, we do not sell the extras to buy what he lacks. We leave him **two of the many and whatever he has of the few**. One might think that until now he borrowed what he lacked in exchange for lending his extras, and that once the gizbar takes them no one will lend to him. The Mishnah teaches that he is no worse off (Bartenura, Rambam). Tosafos Yom Tov notes that food and clothing are different, since he could not manage without them at all.

**"המקדיש את נכסיו מעלין לו את תפיליו."** Someone who consecrated **all** his property loses even his tefillin: they are part of what he consecrated. There are three readings of מעלין. Rashi: they are **assessed**, and he redeems them. Tosafos Yom Tov, following Rambam: they are **taken away from him**, as in the Gemara's "סליקו ליה תפיליו". Yachin: they are **sold at auction** for the most they will fetch. The difference from *arachin* is about intent. A man who consecrates "all my property" thinks of it as a mitzvah and includes his tefillin. A man who pledged an *erech* never meant to give up his tefillin (Tosafos Yom Tov, from Bava Kamma 102b).

# 6:5
**What is not his.** Neither one who consecrates his property nor one who pledges his own *erech* can include his **wife's or children's clothing**. Nor can he include clothes **dyed** for them, or new **sandals** bought for them even if not yet worn. These already belong to them (Bartenura). Tosafos Yom Tov takes צבע to mean dyed garments. Yachin argues it means the **dye** itself. Not even the added value from the dyeing can be claimed.

**"Hekdesh has only its place and its time."** A slave is worth more dressed in fine clothes. A cow fetches more if held for **market day**. A pearl sells higher in the **big city**. Even so, hekdesh is paid by **this place and this time**: ונתן את הערכך **ביום ההוא**. Rambam explains why. Holding goods back for a better price might cause a loss instead, which is why we do not trade with hekdesh funds (Shekalim 4). This applies only to **movables**. Land, as in 6:1, waits sixty days (Tosafos, Yachin). Yachin adds that dressing a slave nicely for sale is not forbidden "deception" (*pirkus*), since it hides no real defect.

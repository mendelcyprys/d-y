# intro
The perek opens with the **minimum and maximum *erech***: one sela and fifty sela'im. "Since it is speaking of 'no less and no more'", the Mishnah then gathers a list of other halachos with a minimum and maximum (Bartenura, Yachin). The list runs through niddah, *nega'im*, the calendar, the *shtei halechem* and *lechem hapanim*, *milah*, and above all the Temple music. These mishnayos are the Mishnah's main source for the instruments of the Mikdash and the Levite choir.

# 2:1
**The minimum: one sela.** "וכל ערכך יהיה בשקל הקדש" (Vayikra 27:25) teaches that no *erech* is ever assessed below one shekel (= sela), even for the poorest man. **The maximum: fifty**, the largest *erech* in the parshah. The case is a poor man who pledged someone's *erech* and has only a little money. Bartenura gives the standard example of a twenty-year-old, but Yachin and Tosafos Yom Tov note it applies to any *erech*, whether of oneself or of someone else.

**He paid a sela and later became rich.** He is free, because a poor man is judged by his means and he paid in full. **He paid less than a sela.** Nothing has been discharged, so once he is rich he pays the full fifty. Tosafos Yom Tov and Melechet Shlomo cite Tosafos and the Tosefta: even if he paid everything except the last part of the sela, the whole *erech* "rolls back" onto him.

**He has five sela'im.** R' Meir holds it is all or nothing: either the full *erech* or one sela, so he gives one. The Chachamim hold he gives everything he has. The verse about a shekel only sets the floor, and above it he pays "על פי אשר תשיג" (27:8). The halachah follows the Chachamim. Why does the Mishnah repeat "אין בערכין פחות מסלע ולא יותר על חמשים"? The repetition is a *stam* Mishnah following the Chachamim. Its implication is that amounts between one and fifty do exist (Gemara, Tosafos Yom Tov). Yachin adds that this covers *any* amount between them, not only the other fixed *erachin* of 5, 15, 20 and so on.

**"אין פתח בטועה..."** A woman has lost track of her cycle (*to'ah*). She cannot tell whether a sighting fell in her seven days of *niddah* or her eleven days of *zivah*. The Mishnah says her new *pesach*, the point from which she can again count a definite start of *niddah*, comes no sooner than seven clean days and no later than seventeen. Rambam (quoted in full by Tosafos Yom Tov) and Yachin set out the full *baraisa*. If she saw one, two or three days, her *pesach* is 17. Each further day of bleeding lowers it by one. At 13 days she is a certain *zavah*, and the *pesach* is 7, because she needs seven clean days in any case. These numbers do **not** govern when she becomes permitted to her husband. That follows the stricter possibility each time. They only re-establish her cycle (Gemara: "לתקוני נדה").

**"אין בנגעים..."** No *hesger* (quarantine) lasts less than one week, and none lasts more than three weeks. Three weeks occurs only with *nig'ei batim*, house afflictions (Bartenura). Yachin finds the Mishnah hard, since some *nega'im* are declared impure at once (Nega'im 1:3). He explains it as meaning that a person placed in *hesger* cannot be released, whether declared pure or confirmed impure, before a week has passed.

# 2:2
**The calendar.** A lunar month is about 29½ days plus 793 *chalakim* (1/1080ths of an hour). Since a month must be whole days ("חודש ימים"), months alternate between full (30 days) and short (29). The surplus fractions, and the *dechiyos* (postponements) of Rosh Hashanah, sometimes require more full or more short months (Rambam, Yachin, and Tosafos Yom Tov citing Rambam, Hilchos Kiddush HaChodesh 6, 8). The Mishnah: no year has fewer than **four** full months, and "it did not seem right" to the Chachamim to have more than **eight**. Yachin explains the odd wording "לא נראה". Short months are easy to justify, since people assume beis din saw the new moon early. Lengthening months when everyone has already seen the moon is harder, so the Mishnah speaks more cautiously.

**The *shtei halechem*** of Shavuos are eaten on the second or third day after baking. Baking does not override Shabbos or Yom Tov, so they are baked before Yom Tov. When Erev Yom Tov is Shabbos they are baked Friday. **The *lechem hapanim*** is baked Friday, set out Shabbos, and eaten the next Shabbos, which is the ninth day. When the two days of Rosh Hashanah fall on Thursday and Friday it is baked Wednesday and eaten on day eleven. **Milah** is on day 8 at the earliest. The latest is day 12, for a baby born *bein hashmashos* on Friday night with the two days of Rosh Hashanah falling on Sunday and Monday. A *milah* that is late (*shelo bizmanah*) does not override Shabbos or Yom Tov.

# 2:3
**Trumpet blasts.** At least **21** a day: three at the opening of the gates, and nine at each daily *tamid*, three for each section of the Levites' song. The most is **48**, on an Erev Shabbos during Sukkos. That adds the *nisuch hamayim*, the *mussaf*, and the blasts marking the start of Shabbos (Yachin, from Sukkah 5:5). The total can reach 57 when Erev Pesach falls on Shabbos, but that is rare, so the Mishnah does not count it (Bartenura).

**Instruments.** *Nevalim*: 2 to 6. *Chalilim* (flutes): 2 to 12. The flute was played before the *mizbe'ach* on **twelve days**: the two times the *pesach* was slaughtered, the first day of Pesach, Shavuos, and the eight days of Sukkos. Tosafos Yom Tov, from the Gemara, explains that these are the days of full Hallel. Sukkos days differ in their *korbanos*, while the days of Pesach do not. Shabbos is not called *mo'ed*. Rosh Chodesh is not "sanctified" by a ban on work. On Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur "can the King sit on the throne of judgment with the books of life and death open, and Israel sing?" Chanukah has Hallel for the miracle, but no special *korban* and therefore no flute. This flute, part of the *korban*'s song, **overrides Shabbos**, because a *shevus* does not apply in the Mikdash. The flute of the *simchas beis hasho'evah* does not (Bartenura, Yachin).

**A reed pipe**, not copper, because its sound is sweeter. At the close of each section of song **a single pipe** carried the final note alone (*machlik*), which sounds finer (Bartenura, Rashi). Rambam explains *abuv yechidi* as a pipe with one hole.

# 2:4
**Who played the instruments?** **R' Meir:** slaves of the kohanim. He holds that serving on the *duchan* is not taken as proof of *yichus* (pure lineage) or of the right to receive *ma'aser*, so it did not matter who played. **R' Yosei:** pedigreed Israelite families whose daughters married kohanim. He holds that the *duchan* *does* serve as proof of *yichus*, so only pedigreed men were allowed up. **R' Chanina ben Antigenos:** leviim. He holds that it serves as proof of the right to *ma'aser*. They disagree **only about the instruments**. Everyone agrees that only leviim sang with their **mouths** over the *korban*. ושרת בשם ה' אלקיו (Devarim 18:7) refers to song. Tosafos Yom Tov notes that the plain meaning of that verse is about kohanim, as Rashi writes on the Chumash. The halachah follows **R' Yosei**.

# 2:5
**Six inspected lambs.** The *tamid* lambs must be checked for blemishes **four days** before slaughter, like the *pesach* in Egypt, which was taken on the 10th and slaughtered on the 14th. That makes 8 lambs in the chamber at all times, and the Mishnah's "six" counts what remains after the day's two are taken. Bartenura and Rambam derive the four days from במועדו / במועדו. Tosafos Yom Tov asks how the *pesach* of the *midbar* proves anything about the Egyptian one. He shows the *baraisa* of Ben Bag Bag learns it from שמירה–שמירה. "**For Shabbos and the two days of Rosh Hashanah**" is only a mnemonic: three days in a row when no lamb can be bought, so four days of reserve are always needed (Bartenura). Yachin explains why Rosh Hashanah is the example. In the days of the Mikdash it was the only time three days of *shabbaton* could fall together.

**Trumpets: at least two**, perhaps because Moshe made "two silver trumpets" (Tosafos Yom Tov). "**Without limit**" here means up to **120**, as in the dedication of Shlomo's Temple, "מאה ועשרים מחצצרים" (Divrei Hayamim II 5:12). More than that would only muddle the sound (Tosafos). **Kinoros:** at least nine. **The tziltzal (cymbal):** only one, meaning one pair struck together and played by one man (Bartenura). Sefer HaChinuch, quoted by Melechet Shlomo, explains why there was only one: it is so loud it would drown out the other instruments and the singing.

# 2:6
**Twelve leviim on the *duchan***, the raised platform with three steps. Bartenura and Tosafos count them as 9 *kinoros*, 2 *nevalim* and 1 cymbal. Rambam (Hilchos Klei HaMikdash 3) holds the twelve are **singers**. That fits the halachah that the main song is vocal, and Tosafos Yom Tov raises this very difficulty with Bartenura's reading.

**Young leviim** were allowed into the Azarah only while the leviim stood singing. Their thin, clear voices "added seasoning" (*tavlin*) to the deep voices of the adults. They sang with their voices only, not with instruments. Rambam reads "אין קטן נכנס..." differently: a new man begins his service only at the time of the song. Yachin and Kesef Mishneh say the wording leans toward Rambam.

**R' Eliezer ben Yaakov:** the young ones do not count toward the twelve and do not stand on the *duchan*. They stand on the ground with their heads between the leviim's feet, and are called **צוערי הלוים**. Rashi and Bartenura explain the name: they "pained" (*tza'ar*) the adults, who could not sweeten their voices as much. Yachin understands it the other way round. The boys pushed the men to lift their voices, since the blending of the two is what gives the song its beauty. In Rambam's reading, R' Eliezer ben Yaakov is speaking of the **instrumentalists**, who do not count among the twelve singers.

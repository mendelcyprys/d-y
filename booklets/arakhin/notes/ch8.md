# intro
Perek 8 has two parts. The first (8:1–3) covers the **auction** of a consecrated *sedeh achuzah* in a time when Yovel is not in force, so the field is redeemed at its **value**. It is an exercise in the owner's added **fifth** (*chomesh*). The second (8:4–7) covers **cherem**, devoting property (Vayikra 27:28): what may be devoted, who may devote, whether an unspecified cherem goes to the kohanim or to *bedek habayis*, and devoting one's *kodashim* and a *bechor*.

Yachin summarises three categories:
- **Achuzah when Yovel is in force:** fixed price. The owners are not compelled to bid first. If unredeemed, it goes to the kohanim.
- **Achuzah when Yovel is not in force:** redeemed at value. The owners are made to bid first, and it never goes to the kohanim.
- **Mikneh:** always redeemed at value, with no fifth and no compulsion.

# 8:1
**"In a time when there is no Yovel."** Yovel stopped once **Reuven, Gad and half of Menashe were exiled**. The Sifra reads וקראתם דרור בארץ **לכל יושביה** as "only when all its inhabitants are on it" (Bartenura, Rambam). Tosafos adds that the case is while the Mikdash stood, when hekdesh actually gains something.

**"You open first."** The owners are asked to make the opening bid for three reasons (Gemara, Bartenura, Rambam):
1. They add a **fifth**, which means a quarter of the base, so the base plus the fifth is five parts.
2. The **mitzvah of redemption** falls on the owner (ואם לא **יגאל**).
3. "A man prefers one *kav* of his own to nine of someone else's", so he will pay more.

**The story.** A man consecrated a field because it was poor, costing more to work than it yielded. Told to "open first", he said, "It's mine for an **issar**" (eight *perutos*). R' Yosei says he actually offered **an egg**, since *hekdesh* can be redeemed with money's worth. The gizbar said "**it's yours**": he lost an issar and kept his field. The dispute behind the story (Bartenura, Rambam): the Chachamim hold *hekdesh* is never redeemed for less than **four perutos**, so that its fifth is at least a *perutah*. R' Yosei holds any amount is enough. The halachah follows the Chachamim. Yachin notes that the story teaches the four-*perutah* minimum holds even for a worthless field.

**Today**, with no Mikdash, *hekdesh* may be redeemed even at the outset for a token sum. The Sages set about **four zuz** so the redemption is publicly known, and the money is **thrown into the river** (Rambam, end of Hilchos Arachin; Tosafos Yom Tov).

# 8:2
**Bidders who retract.** One bids 10, another 20, then 30, 40, 50. If the 50-bidder retracts, we **take collateral** from him for 10, the difference down to the next bid. The same applies all the way down. If the 10-bidder retracts, the field is sold at its value and he pays the shortfall. His words bind him because "a statement to the Most High is like delivery to a layman" (Tosafos Yom Tov, Kiddushin 1:6). This applies when they retracted **one after another**. If several retract **together**, the loss is shared. Bartenura and Rambam give one way of dividing it. Rashi and Ra'avad give another (Yachin).

**Owners and an outsider both bid 20.** The **owners come first**, since they add a fifth.

# 8:3
**The owners bid 20, and with the fifth that comes to 25.** An outsider bids **21**. Giving it to him loses hekdesh 4, since 21 is less than 25. Giving it to the owners at 25 loses hekdesh **base**, since 21 is more than 20. So the owners are compelled to pay **21 + 5 = 26**. The fifth is added only on **their own** 20, not on the outsider's extra sela. In the same way, 22 gives 27, 23 gives 28, and 24 gives 29. **At 25** the owners still pay **30**. Why not let the outsider have it, when his 25 equals their 25? The Gemara says the owners must have bid "20 and a *perutah*", and the Mishnah does not bother with *perutos* (Bartenura, Yachin).

**An outsider bids 26.** If the owners had opened at **21**, their total with the fifth was 26 sela'im and **a dinar** (a sela being 4 dinarim). The outsider's 26 is short of that, so the owners must redeem: 26 base plus the fifth of their own 21, which is 5 sela'im and a dinar, giving **31 sela'im and a dinar**. "אם רצו" refers to what they wanted **at the start** (Gemara). If they had opened at only 20, we say to the outsider "**הגיעתך**": it's yours. Yachin explains why it cannot mean the owners can choose *now* to pay 31 and a dinar. If they took it now they would owe a fifth on the full 26.

If **three experts** assessed the field at the outsider's figure, the owners add a fifth on **all** of it (Bartenura, Rambam). This whole mishnah concerns an *achuzah* when Yovel is not in force. With a *mikneh*, the owners are never compelled (Rambam).

# 8:4
**Cherem: only part.** A person may devote **some** of his sheep and cattle, his Canaanite slaves, and his *sedeh achuzah*. The verse says **מ**כל אשר לו, and the **מ** means "from", so part. **R' Elazar:** if he devoted **all** of them, the cherem does not take effect. The Gemara explains why each of the three categories is needed. **R' Elazar ben Azaryah** gives a *kal vachomer*. If a person may not devote everything even to the Most High, how much more must he spare his property and not squander it on ordinary purposes.

The Gemara gives the practical difference: **R' Ila's** rule at Usha, "one who gives generously should not give more than a **fifth**". That follows Yaakov's עשר אעשרנו, two tenths. The halachah: ideally one should not devote everything, but if he did, it takes effect (Bartenura, Rambam). Rambam, at the end of Hilchos Arachin, writes that one who gives away all his property is "not pious but foolish". Tosafos asks how a cherem for kohanim counts as "to the Most High". They answer that while it is still in the owner's house it carries *kedushas haguf* (holiness of the object itself).

# 8:5
**What one cannot devote.** His son or daughter, his Hebrew slave or maidservant, and his *sedeh mikneh*: "a person cannot devote what is not his". The Gemara compares this to the animal in the verse: only what he could sell **forever**. A father may sell a minor daughter, but not once she is a *na'arah* (Bartenura). A Hebrew slave's body is acquired, but his master cannot resell him (Yachin).

**Kohanim and leviim.** **R' Yehudah:** neither may devote. **R' Shimon:** kohanim may not, because the *charamim* are theirs (כל חרם בישראל לך יהיה) and he would simply acquire it himself. Leviim may, because the *charamim* are not theirs. **Rebbi:** R' Yehudah is right about **land**, which even R' Shimon accepts, since כי אחזת עולם הוא להם. R' Shimon is right about **movables**. Bartenura says the halachah follows R' Shimon, as Rebbi went out of his way to explain him. Tosafos Yom Tov notes that Rambam's code follows **R' Yehudah**, by the usual rule. An accurate text of Rambam's commentary reads "therefore the halachah follows R' Yehudah", not "does not".

# 8:6
**Charmei kohanim**, things devoted for kohanim, have no redemption and are simply given to the kohanim (לא ימכר ולא יגאל). Things devoted to *bedek habayis* are redeemed, since the Temple fund needs money. **An unspecified cherem:** **R' Yehudah ben Beseira** says it goes to *bedek habayis*: כל חרם קדש קדשים הוא לה'. The **Chachamim** say it goes to the **kohanim**: כשדה **החרם** לכהן תהיה אחזתו. They read the first verse as teaching that cherem takes effect even on *kodshei kodashim* and *kodashim kalim* (8:7). The halachah follows the Chachamim (Bartenura, Rambam). Kesef Mishneh leaves this as *tzarich iyun*, since the Gemara seems to favour R' Yehudah ben Beseira.

**Today**, an unspecified cherem of movables, or of land outside Eretz Yisrael, goes to the kohanim. Something consecrated to *bedek habayis* is redeemed for about four zuz (8:1). Boaz explains why the language of *hekdesh* and *cherem* follows **the Torah's** usage rather than everyday speech, unlike ordinary *nedarim*.

# 8:7
**Devoting one's own *kodashim*.** If it was a **neder** ("הרי עלי עולה", for which he is responsible), he pays its **full value** to the kohen and still offers the animal for his vow. If it was a **nedavah** ("הרי זו עולה", for which he is not responsible), he pays only its **tovas hana'ah** (the benefit of choosing whom to give it to). That is what a person under no obligation would pay for the chance to offer this ox as a gift to his Creator. Bartenura explains אינו רשאי as "not obligated" (from the Targum of נושה, רשיא).

**A *bechor***, blemished or not, may also be devoted. Its *tovas hana'ah* is assessed as what someone would pay the owner to give it to **his daughter's son or his sister's son who is a kohen**. A kohen may not pay for it himself. That would make him like a kohen who helps at the threshing floor to be given *terumah*, receiving it as wages and not as a gift (Bartenura, Yachin).

**R' Yishmael:** one verse says **תקדיש** (Devarim 15:19) and another says **לא יקדיש** (Vayikra 27:26). So the *bechor* is consecrated as **הקדש עילוי**, for its added value, but not as a different altar-offering. The Chachamim learn this from כל חרם קדש קדשים. They use the verses differently. **תקדיש** is a mitzvah to declare it holy as a *bechor*, though it is holy from the womb anyway. **לא יקדיש** is a *lav* against designating it as another *korban*. The halachah follows the Chachamim.

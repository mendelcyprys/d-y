# intro
The last perek leaves *hekdesh* for **ordinary sales** under the Yovel system (Vayikra 25:14–34). It contrasts three kinds of property:
- **Fields** may not be redeemed for two years, after which they are redeemed at a reduced price. They return at Yovel.
- **Houses in walled cities** can be redeemed only within one year. After that they belong to the buyer permanently.
- **Houses in unwalled villages** (*batei chatzerim*) get the advantages of both.

The tractate closes with the special rules for **Levite cities**: "גאלת עולם תהיה ללוים".

# 9:1
**A field sold while Yovel is in force** may not be redeemed **within two years**, even if the buyer agrees: במספר שני תבואות ימכר לך. Both parties would transgress an *asei* (Bartenura; Rambam, Hilchos Shemittah 11). Selling **in the Yovel year itself** is void, by *kal vachomer*: if a field already sold goes back, one not yet sold certainly cannot be sold (Gemara). After two years the seller may redeem **against the buyer's will**, paying back the price **prorated by the years remaining**. Yachin's example: a field sold for 20 sela'im with ten years to Yovel comes to 2 a year. After four years the seller pays back 12.

**Years that do not count:** a year of **blight** (*shidafon*, *yerakon*) or *shemittah*. The verse says "years of **produce**". Tosafos adds that the blight must be general, like *shemittah*. **Years that do count:** if the buyer only ploughed (*nirah*) or left it fallow (*hovirah*), the loss is his own. Yachin explains why the Mishnah names both cases. **R' Elazar:** if he sold it before Rosh Hashanah full of crops, the buyer can enjoy **three harvests in two years**. That follows from "years of produce" rather than "years" or "harvests". Bartenura and Rambam say this is not a dispute but agreed by all.

# 9:2
**Calculating with resales.** If A sold to B for a *maneh* and B sold to C for two hundred, A calculates **with B's price**: לאיש אשר מכר לו. If A sold for two hundred and B resold for a *maneh*, A calculates **with C's price**: לאיש, the one who is now **master** of the field. Tosafos Yom Tov compares אישי כהן גדול (Yoma 1:3). Either way the verses are read **in the seller's favour**. This is learned from גאולה–גאולה with the Hebrew slave, where the redemption follows the cheaper figure (מכסף מקנתו / כפי שניו). Rav Nachman expounded those verses "like Sinai".

**What the seller may not do** (from והשיגה ידו ומצא כדי גאולתו):
- **sell a distant field to redeem a near one**, or a poor one to redeem a good one, because ומצא means newly found means
- **borrow** to redeem
- **redeem half**, because כדי גאולתו means the whole

Tosafos and Rambam note it is equally forbidden the other way round, selling the near to redeem the far. **With *hekdesh*, all of these are permitted**, including redeeming half (7:1). "This is a stringency of an ordinary sale over *hekdesh*."

# 9:3
**A house in a walled city** may be redeemed **at once**, and only **within twelve months**. "It is like interest, but not interest." If he returns the full price within the year, the buyer has lived in the house for free in return for the use of his money. It is not real *ribis*, because this is a **sale** that may become permanent, not a loan (Bartenura). Yachin explains it as *tzad echad beribis* (a deal that turns out to be interest only in one of its possible outcomes). Rabbinically that is *avak ribis*, but the Torah itself permits it here. Boaz asks how this fits the Taz's principle that the Sages cannot forbid what the Torah explicitly permits.

**If the seller dies, his son redeems. If the buyer dies, the seller redeems from his son.** The year runs **from the first sale**, even if the buyer resold it: עד מלאת **לו**. A **leap year** gives thirteen months (תמימה). **Rebbi** says it is always a solar year: 354 days plus 11. The halachah follows the Chachamim.

# 9:4
**Unredeemed by the end of the year**, the house belongs permanently to the buyer, and to a recipient of a **gift** as well, since לצמיתות is plural (Yachin). Onkelos renders it לחלוטין, "with finality" (Rambam).

**Hillel's *takanah*.** Buyers used to **hide** on the last day so the seller could not pay them. Hillel HaZaken enacted that the seller may **deposit the money in the Temple chamber**, break open the door, and enter. The buyer collects his money whenever he likes. This rests on *hefker beis din hefker*: beis din can transfer ownership (Tosafos Yom Tov).

# 9:5
**What counts as "inside the wall".** Everything within the wall has the status of a walled-city house: olive presses, bathhouses, towers, cisterns, caves (אשר בעיר). **Fields** are excluded, since the verse says בית. R' Meir includes "fields", which Bartenura explains as rocky or sandy plots used for building materials. **A house built into the wall:** R' Yehudah says it does not count, R' Shimon that its outer wall is the city wall. Both learn from Rachav: כי ביתה בקיר החומה **ובחומה היא יושבת**. The halachah follows R' Yehudah (Bartenura, Rambam).

# 9:6
**What counts as a walled city.** A town whose **rooftops form its wall** (houses joined in a ring) does not count. Neither does one **not walled since the days of Yehoshua bin Nun**. A town that was walled then counts **even if it has no wall now**. The Torah writes אשר **לא** חומה but reads it **לו**: "although it has none now, it once had one". Tosafos Yom Tov notes that our precise Chumashim read it so: written לא, read לו. The Masoretic text here agrees (Vayikra 25:30). A "city" means at least **three courtyards of two houses each**.

**The list** includes the old Kitzrah of Tzippori, the Chakrah of Gush Chalav, old Yodfas, Gamla, Gadud, Chadid, Ono, Yerushalayim, and others like them. The Gemara (R' Yishmael b'R' Yosei) explains why these are listed. When the exiles returned they found these walled and consecrated them. The first sanctity of the land had lapsed, so for the Second Temple period only cities walled in Ezra's time count (Rambam, Shemittah 12). **Yerushalayim:** a house in the holy city never becomes permanent, since it was never divided among the tribes. The Gemara therefore suggests the list refers to **another, smaller Yerushalayim** (Bartenura, Yachin).

# 9:7
**Batei chatzerim**, houses in unwalled villages, get "**the better side of both**". Like walled-city houses, they may be redeemed **immediately**. Like fields, they may be redeemed **at any time**, return **at Yovel**, and are redeemed **with a reduction for years used**. This is from גאלה תהיה לו / על שדה הארץ יחשב. **Two courtyards of two houses** count as a village **even if walled since Yehoshua's time**. The verse adds "אשר אין להם חומה", which teaches that some places have a wall but are treated as if they had none.

# 9:8
**Levite cities.** A Yisrael who inherited a walled-city house from his **Levite mother's father**, or a Levi who inherited one from his **Yisrael mother's father**, does not redeem by the Levite rule. **Rebbi** says the rule requires both a **Levi** and a **Levite city**: כי בתי ערי **הלוים**, together with ואשר יגאל **מן הלוים**. The **Chachamim** say it depends only on the **city**. The halachah follows the Chachamim.

**The open belt** (*migrash*) around a city must stay as it is. A field may not be made into *migrash*, nor *migrash* into a field. *Migrash* may not be built up into city, nor city cleared into *migrash*. The verse ושדה מגרש עריהם לא ימכר means "not changed" (Bartenura). **R' Eliezer:** this is in Levite cities. In Israelite cities one may turn a field into *migrash*, because a beautiful town draws settlers. One may not turn *migrash* into a field, "so as not to ruin the cities of Israel". The commentators disagree on how to read this. Bartenura and Tosafos Yom Tov take R' Eliezer as explaining the Tanna Kamma. Yachin takes it as a dispute. Rambam rules the Israelite cities are like the Levite ones, and not like R' Eliezer.

**Kohanim and leviim sell at any time and redeem at any time.** They may sell even close to Yovel, though not in the Yovel year itself (Kesef Mishneh). They may redeem fields immediately, and their walled-city houses never become permanent: גאלת עולם תהיה ללוים. Kohanim are included, since they too are "הכהנים הלוים" (Yechezkel 44:15).

**סליקא לה מסכת ערכין.**

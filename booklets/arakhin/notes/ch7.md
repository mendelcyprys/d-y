# intro
Perek 7 works through Vayikra 27:16–24: **consecrating and redeeming fields**. The *sedeh achuzah* (an inherited field) is redeemed at a fixed price of 50 shekels per *beis kor* for a full Yovel cycle, prorated "according to the years that remain". If no one redeems it, it goes to the **kohanim** at Yovel. The *sedeh mikneh* (a bought field) goes back to its original owner. The arithmetic: 50 sela'im over 49 years comes to "**a sela and a pundyon a year**". A sela is 48 pundyonim, so 49 × (1 sela + 1 pundyon) = 49 sela'im + 49 pundyonim, one pundyon more than 50 sela'im. The extra pundyon is a *kalbon*, the money-changer's premium for paying in small coins (Bartenura, Rambam from the Sifra). Tosafos Yom Tov notes, from Tosafos, that the word *kalbon* is used loosely here.

# 7:1
**"אין מקדישין לפני היובל פחות משתי שנים."** This is **advice**, not an invalidity. Such a *hekdesh* takes effect, but "according to the years that remain" requires at least two years ("the minimum of 'years' is two"). With less than two years left there is no deduction, so he would have to pay the full **50 sela'im** to redeem it (Bartenura, Rambam, Yachin). Tosafos explains why the Mishnah does not also advise against consecrating in the **first** year after Yovel. He could still redeem then by the yearly rate.

**"ולא גואלין לאחר היובל פחות משנה."** Bartenura and Rambam explain this following Rav. "After the Yovel" means any time in the cycle. Someone redeeming in the middle of a year pays the **whole** year's sela and pundyon, since "you count years, not months". Yachin and Tosafos Yom Tov explain it following **Shmuel**. A Yisrael who consecrates *during the Yovel year itself* achieves nothing, so no redemption is needed in that year. Rambam rules like Shmuel (Arachin 4:10).

**"אין מחשבין חדשים להקדש, אבל ההקדש מחשב חדשים."** The redeemer cannot count part-years in his favour. Hekdesh can count them in *its* favour. Example: halfway through year 48 there are less than two full years left, so the full 50 is due rather than two sela'im and two pundyonim. The source is וחשב לו הכהן, "in any case" (Bartenura). Rambam and Ra'avad hold the gizbar may choose either way (Yachin).

**"בשעת היובל"** means when Yovel is in force. When it is not (8:1), the field is redeemed at its **value** like any *hekdesh*. The area is measured by sowing barley **by hand at an average density**. **Hollows 10 *tefachim* deep or rocks 10 high** are not measured with the field. Less than that counts as part of the field. The Gemara limits the hollows to ones **filled with water**, which cannot be sown (בית **זרע**). Hollows that are not filled with water are disputed. Rambam and Kesef Mishneh: they are consecrated on their own and assessed at their value. Rashi and Tosafos (Kiddushin 61a): they are measured on their own at the same rate, but without counting the slope. Tosafos Yom Tov discusses Bartenura's wording at length.

**He must pay all at once**, not year by year: וחשב לו... **את הכסף**, as a single sum. But he may redeem **half** the field (9:2).

# 7:2
**Owner or anyone.** Anyone may redeem an *achuzah* at the same fixed price, whether the field is worth a thousand or five. The only difference is that the **owner adds a fifth**: ואם גאול יגאל את השדה **המקדיש אותו** ויסף חמישית (27:19). Rambam's example: with eight years left, the owner pays 8 sela'im and 8 pundyonim plus a fifth, which is **10 sela'im and 10 pundyonim** per *chomer*. The fifth is "added from outside", a quarter of the base (Yachin).

# 7:3
**Who redeemed it decides what happens at Yovel.**
- **The owner redeemed it:** it stays his and does not go out at Yovel.
- **His son redeemed it:** it returns to the **father** at Yovel. The verse says ואם מכר את השדה לאיש **אחר**, so a son is not "another". Why include the son rather than the brother? The son steps into his father's place for *yi'ud* (designating the father's Hebrew maidservant as a wife). The brother's place is for *yibum*, and *yibum* exists only where there is no son (Gemara, Tosafos Yom Tov).
- **Someone else or a relative redeemed it, and the owner redeemed it back from him:** our texts (Bartenura, Rambam) read "**it does not leave him**". The Gemara's text, Rashi, Ra'avad and Tosafos read "**it does leave**": he bought it from a third party, so at Yovel it goes to the kohanim. Tosafos Yom Tov leans to the second reading as fitting the verse better.
- **A kohen redeemed it and still holds it:** he cannot say "it goes to the kohanim at Yovel, and it's in my hand, so it's mine". It is shared among **all the kohanim** of the *mishmar*, because לכהן תהיה **אחוזתו** speaks of his own inherited land, and this is not that (*baraisa*).

# 7:4
**Yovel came and no one redeemed it.** **R' Yehudah:** the kohanim of the *mishmar* on duty enter it and **pay its value**, the fifty shekels. Tosafos Yom Tov calls the "three shekels" in Kesef Mishneh's text a scribal slip (נ' misread as ג'). The money goes to *bedek habayis*. He learns קדש–קדש from **a consecrated house**: both are *bedek habayis* property, and a house never leaves hekdesh without payment. **R' Shimon:** they enter **without paying**. He learns from the **Shavuos lambs** (קדש יהיו לה' לכהן): both are among the 24 gifts to kohanim. **R' Eliezer:** they neither enter nor pay. The verse בצאתו ביובל speaks of the field leaving **a redeemer's hand** at Yovel. Until someone redeems it, it is called **שדה רטושים**, an "abandoned field", through the second Yovel and the third. Only after someone redeems it and Yovel comes does it go to the kohanim. Yachin says R' Eliezer had not received the *gezeirah shavah*. The halachah follows **R' Yehudah**.

Tosafos Yom Tov explains the name. *Retushim* (Hoshea 10:14, אם על בנים רוטשה) is property abandoned **by choice**, unlike *netushim*, property abandoned under compulsion (Bava Metzia 39a). The owner consecrated this field willingly. The label also makes the field's status known to all, so that whoever later redeems it knows it will go to the kohanim.

# 7:5
**A field bought from one's father.** If the father died and *then* the son consecrated it, it is an *achuzah*: it had already come to him by inheritance. If he consecrated it and *then* the father died, **R' Meir** says it is a *mikneh*, because we look at the time of the *hekdesh*. **R' Yehudah and R' Shimon** say it is an *achuzah*. The verse defines a *mikneh* as one אשר לא **משדה** אחוזתו, a field **not fit to become** an *achuzah*. This one was fit, since he stood to inherit it. Rashi learns this from the extra word משדה, Rashbam from the extra מ'. The halachah does not follow R' Meir.

**A *sedeh mikneh* does not go to the kohanim at Yovel**, "because a person cannot consecrate what is not his". He owned the field only until Yovel. It goes back to its original owner. Rashi on the Chumash says this happens even if it is still in the gizbar's hand (Tosafos Yom Tov).

**Kohanim and leviim consecrate at any time and redeem at any time**, even in the Yovel year and even after Yovel has passed. A Yisrael's field that was not redeemed before Yovel can no longer be redeemed (לא יגאל עוד). For leviim the verse says גאלת עולם תהיה ללוים (Vayikra 25:32; Bartenura). The printed Rambam commentary remarks that a Yisrael can also consecrate at any time, "even in the Yovel year itself". Tosafos Yom Tov reports that in his accurate manuscript those words are crossed out, which fits Rambam's ruling like Shmuel.
